# Homepage, Category Pages, and Taxonomy

## Homepage job

The homepage should quickly communicate:

1. what the store sells;
2. who it is for/why it is credible;
3. how to begin finding products;
4. current meaningful promotions or categories;
5. how to get help/understand fulfillment.

It should not be a slideshow repository.

## Above-the-fold priorities

Depending on brand/vertical:

- clear brand/store identity;
- search;
- primary category paths;
- one clear value proposition or current campaign;
- evidence of product range.

New visitors should not need to scroll through brand storytelling before they can tell what is sold.

## Category exposure

Expose representative top-level categories through navigation and/or visual category cards.

Category cards should use:

- meaningful image/icon when useful;
- explicit category label;
- real link;
- image that accurately represents the category.

Do not use abstract lifestyle images so similar that customers cannot distinguish categories.

## Curated discovery

Useful modules may include:

- best sellers;
- new arrivals;
- seasonal collections;
- shop by need/use case;
- shop by brand;
- reorder for known customers;
- editorial buying guidance.

Every module should have a clear scope and route to "view all" when needed.

## Carousels

Use sparingly.

If a carousel exists:

- essential content should not be trapped only in later slides;
- controls are visible and keyboard accessible;
- swipe works on touch;
- pause auto-rotation on interaction;
- avoid auto-rotation on mobile as a default;
- no rapid motion;
- honor reduced-motion preferences.

A static hero or grid is often easier to understand.

## Taxonomy design

A taxonomy is a customer navigation model, not just category database rows.

Evaluate:

- top-level category names;
- mutually understandable groupings;
- depth;
- cross-category products;
- category-specific attributes;
- URL/breadcrumb path.

### Category depth

Prefer fewer meaningful levels over long chains.

Bad:

```text
Products > Home > Kitchen > Appliances > Small Appliances > Beverage Appliances > Coffee > Coffee Makers
```

if several middle levels add no browsing value.

## Intermediary category pages

Use when a category contains distinct subgroups that benefit from orientation before a product list.

Example:

```text
Electronics
- Phones
- Laptops
- TVs
- Audio
- Accessories
```

An intermediary page should help route users, not simply repeat the mega-menu in a huge blank screen.

## Category page vs PLP

Distinguish:

- **Category landing page**: navigation/orientation, may link to subcategories and editorial content.
- **PLP**: actual list/grid of purchasable products with filtering/sorting.

Do not force a routing page when the category is already narrow enough for a useful PLP.

## Empty categories

Do not expose navigation routes that lead to permanently empty dead ends.

If temporarily empty:

- explain no products currently available;
- offer parent/sibling categories;
- optionally show relevant alternatives;
- choose SEO/index behavior intentionally.

## Cross-navigation

On deep category/PLP, provide routes to related sibling/parent scopes without forcing a full return to home.

## Personalization

Personalization may improve discovery, but:

- do not remove universal browsing/search;
- don't infer sensitive preferences unnecessarily;
- provide fallback for new/anonymous users;
- avoid layouts that shift unpredictably during loading.

## Homepage/category acceptance checks

- [ ] A new visitor can identify what the store sells quickly.
- [ ] Search/browse paths are obvious.
- [ ] Major category labels are differentiated and understandable.
- [ ] Visual category tiles map to real categories.
- [ ] Carousel does not hide essential content or auto-rotate aggressively.
- [ ] Category pages route users without unnecessary layers.
- [ ] Empty scopes provide recovery paths.
- [ ] Breadcrumb/navigation hierarchy matches customer taxonomy.
